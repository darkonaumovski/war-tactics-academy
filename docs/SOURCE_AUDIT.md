# Historical source audit

Checked 23 October 2026. The eight analyses in `data/research.json` are concise editorial syntheses, not transcriptions. Each contains the 14 requested analysis categories and four chronological phases. Cannae, Alesia, Hastings, Austerlitz, Waterloo, Midway and Stalingrad include opposing command perspectives. Those perspectives explicitly distinguish documented actions from reconstructed constraints or reasoning.

## Editorial rules used

- `fact` means an event or claim supported by the cited source set. Where ancient evidence is an author's report, the prose says so; the label does not make that author's account infallible.
- `interpretation` identifies the academy's explanatory synthesis or learning question. It is not a purported quotation, private thought or precise intelligence record.
- `uncertainty` makes material limitations visible.
- Phase times are broad chronological labels. These are schematic teaching sequences, not georeferenced unit positions, ship tracks or precise reconstructed movement orders.
- No numerical casualties, invented direct quotations, guessed troop coordinates or numerical commander ratings are included.
- No remote media has been copied. Source links are reading references, not licenses to reuse publisher imagery.
- Source pages can themselves contain simplified or disputed claims. For example, the Waterloo analysis does not adopt broad claims of uninterrupted European peace after 1815; the Austerlitz analysis avoids incompatible casualty totals.

## Remaining editorial work

A specialist review should expand claim-level citations, add contrasting scholarship, and audit primary-text translations. All 140 analyses are deliberately concise. None lists fewer than four sources, but 62 list only four, many of them encyclopedic or institutional, rather than the primary texts and specialist histories behind the first eight, and must not be presented as having the same depth of verification. Modern scholarship and archaeological reports should precede any future conversion of schematic positions into geographic overlays.

Ten of the volume's twelve lessons are now published.

Corrections made while writing:

- The coordination lesson placed Vlamertinghe "west of Ypres", which no source read says; removed. It called Pershing's letter of 31 August a letter of "objections"; Foch calls them observations. It said the British were driven "towards the Channel"; Britannica says towards the sea. It said the record shows Foch keeping both partners' trust; it now says only that he went on to direct the summer's offensives.
- The Amiens lesson cited Britannica's Foch article for Haig's judgement in 1917; the reference is now the Passchendaele article. Its counter-case placed Ludendorff's May breakthrough "on the Aisne"; Britannica says the French front.
- The general-battle lesson said Foch's instruction of 3 September ordered every Allied army to attack; it went to Pershing and Pétain, and the lesson now quotes Foch's earlier aim of bringing all the Allied armies into the battle. It called the 2018 meta-analysis's lead author "her"; no source read gives a gender, and the text now says "Lee and colleagues". It called Monash's critics his divisional commanders; Pedersen names Hobbs and others.

Counter-cases: Pershing (coordination), Ludendorff (Amiens, his third appearance in the volume, each on a different decision: his record, his tactics, his refusal to switch) and Monash (the general battle). The Ypres episode links the First Ypres study.
