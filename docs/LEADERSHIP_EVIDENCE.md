# Leadership Lessons from Napoleon: evidence pass

Phase 0 record, 9 November 2026. It grades whether each planned lesson can be carried by sources that were actually opened. It will be folded into `docs/SOURCE_AUDIT.md` when Phase 0 is committed.

**How the reading was done.** Long memoirs were searched in full text for the passages each lesson depends on; they were not read from cover to cover. Web articles were read through a page-reading tool that returns a summary, so exact wording must be re-read before it is quoted or cited in a lesson. Psychology papers were read as abstracts only unless noted. Page numbers come from running heads in the scanned text and need checking against the scan.

Grades: **strong** means the lesson's main claims and its counter-evidence are in hand. **Adequate** means the argument can be made but named gaps remain. **Thin** means the lesson must be redrawn, merged or dropped.

## Corrections to the plan

1. **Escalation of commitment is contested (lesson 13).** The prompt offered Staw 1976 as settled. A 2021 replication (Białek, Węgrzyn and Meyers, N = 365) found no evidence of escalation; the FLoRA atlas also lists a mixed 2022 replication by Schultze and Schulz-Hardt. Sleesman et al. (2012) is a meta-analysis of many determinants. The lesson must present this as an unsettled finding, and it fits the lesson better that way: Caulaincourt says Napoleon had no illusions about Moscow yet stayed, which is hard to file under a laboratory bias.
2. **The Malet story is more complicated (lesson 17).** A popular account says nobody thought of the heir. Lentz says the dignitaries "appeared reluctant", and warns against exaggerating this. Caulaincourt reports Napoleon complaining that Clarke ran to the barracks to take the oath to the King of Rome. The lesson should treat the reaction as disputed.
3. **The staff's role is itself a disagreement (lessons 9, 10).** Britannica says the staff were required not to think or act independently. Gambiez says Berthier was far more than a transmitter. That makes a good source check.
4. **The directed telescope (lesson 9) is unread.** Redraw the lesson as what a headquarters built to serve one mind could and could not do, or find a readable text.
5. **Four lessons have no read psychology source for the planned mechanism** (1, 8, 9, 10), and several more rest on weak analogies. Each batch must find one, or the psychologist's section must say plainly that no reliable mechanism is offered.
