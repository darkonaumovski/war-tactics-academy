# Product roadmap

The battle expansion is complete: 140 engagements with studies in six collections (Alexander, Rome, Napoleon, the First World War, the Serbian Front and the Second World War), 106 commander profiles, ten courses and nineteen decision exercises, with a readable atlas, collection pages, shareable catalog filters, campaign route maps, commander career strips and search by war, campaign and commander. The priorities below follow from it.

## Priority 1 — Editorial depth and evidence

1. Historian review of all 140 studies and 106 commander profiles. Extend section-level references to sentence-level citations with editions and page locators, add contrasting scholarship, and expand the brief study sections. No study now lists fewer than four sources, but 62 of the 140 list only four, many of them encyclopedic or institutional; the sixteen that listed only two were deepened in four batches (T1–T4), often with a source from the other side, sixteen three-source studies that drew on one side only were deepened in batches D1–D4, and the remaining three-source studies are being taken by period, beginning with the First and Second World Wars (P1–P3) and continuing with the Napoleonic Wars (P4), other periods (P5), Rome (P6) and Alexander (P7), with the last one-sided studies, Verdun, Friedland and Vendémiaire, deepened from memoirs and letters (P8), and the last eleven three-source studies gaining primary texts (P9–P10). Specialist histories and primary texts should come next.
2. Add substantive campaign narratives and war bibliographies, by collection. Batch K1 gave the three Alexander campaigns a sourced narrative and the Alexander war page an annotated bibliography; K2 did the same for Napoleon's eight campaigns from Toulon to 1809 and the French Revolutionary Wars page. Napoleon from 1812 to 1815 (with the Napoleonic Wars bibliography), the First World War with the Serbian Front, the Second World War and Rome come next, then a short context for the thirty one-engagement campaigns outside the collections. The other campaign pages are still indexes with a schematic map.
3. Develop battle-specific interpretive diagrams with documented geometry provenance; use georeferenced overlays only where source quality supports them. Preserve a text equivalent.
4. Review global periodization, command responsibility and transliteration consistently. Expand African, Asian, Middle Eastern and American coverage beyond the European-centred collections. Batches G1–G4 give a battle to each commander profile that had none; G1 added Mohi, Hansan Island, Nagashino and Karnal, G2 Dara, Arsuf, Leuthen and Rymnik, G3 Vicksburg, the Atlanta campaign, Adwa and Pratapgad, and G4 Yehuling for Genghis Khan. Shaka remains unlinked: no reliable account of a battle of his was found (see the source audit).

## Priority 2 — Curriculum and media

1. Specialist review of all ten learning paths, especially the five collection courses added in the expansion.
2. Add deeper quizzes (currently one question per lesson), advanced learning levels and source-criticism exercises; retain feedback rather than performance rankings.
3. Grow the nine-record media library: verified official video embeds beyond D-Day, more public-domain portraits, maps and primary documents, each with full attribution.
4. Give each commander profile and war page its own multi-source bibliography.

## Priority 3 — Product and architecture

1. Reduce per-page payloads and add route-specific projections as the catalog grows; add Open Graph images for shared links.
2. Lighthouse has been run on the published site (see the verification record). Next: cut the battle catalog's rendering cost and payload on phones, then profile Core Web Vitals in the field; test with screen readers and high zoom.
3. Add progress export/import, optional accounts and cloud synchronization with explicit data ownership and privacy controls.
4. Add an editorial CMS with provenance, corrections, review status and audit history.
5. The static GitHub Pages site needs no runtime database. Migrate to PostgreSQL and a server deployment only if accounts or writes are added, with a backup strategy.

## Later

Teacher/classroom collections, multilingual content, richer campaign playback, achievements, moderated discussion and certificates. None should obscure the boundary between historical evidence and counterfactual teaching.
