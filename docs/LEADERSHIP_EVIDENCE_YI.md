# Leadership Lessons from Yi Sun-sin: evidence pass

Phase 0 record, 25 November 2026, for Volume 10 under `docs/LEADERSHIP_YI_PROMPT.md`, after its studies phase (Okpo, Sacheon, Danghangpo, Busanpo, Chilcheollyang, Myeongnyang and Noryang, `0cda2cb`). It grades whether each planned lesson can be carried by sources actually opened, and traces every saying in the prompt's traps table.

**How the reading was done.** Everything in Phase S is listed in `docs/SOURCE_AUDIT.md` and not repeated here: Yi's reports and diary in the original, the Annals and their revised edition in Korean translation, the encyclopedia's entries on the battles, Yi and Won Gyun, the _Seikan iryaku_, the _Ming shi_, Aston, Ballard, and the abstracts of Ogawa and the _Military History_ article. Read for this pass:

- **The _Record of Conduct_ (行錄)** by Yi's nephew Yi Bun (李芬), _Complete Works_ vol. 9, appendix 1 (`https://db.itkc.or.kr/dir/item?itemId=MO#/dir/node?dataId=ITKC_MO_0232A_0120_010_0010`), in Classical Chinese, in the Korean Classics Database: the passages on Yi's arrest and release, his mother's death, his reappointment, his memorial against abandoning the navy, Myeongnyang, the negotiations with Chen Lin and Noryang.
- **Searches of the Korean Classics Database** for the sayings in the traps table, which located them in the _Record of Conduct_, the _Gukjo bogam_ and the _Jaejo beonbangji_; only the _Record of Conduct_ was read.

Identified but not read: the Annals entries of the 1st–2nd months of 1597 on the order to intercept Katō Kiyomasa, and of the 3rd month on Won Gyun's report of the 28th day of the 2nd month (both located by search); the _Gukjo bogam_ and _Jaejo beonbangji_ passages on Yi's death; the full texts of the KCI articles on Noryang, on Won Gyun's reputation and on Arima Seihō's criticism; Ogawa's 2022 notes; the General Staff's _Nihon senshi: Chōsen eki_; any psychology for this volume.

Grades: **strong** means the lesson's main claims and its counter-evidence are in hand. **Adequate** means the argument can be made but named gaps remain. **Thin** means the lesson must be redrawn, merged or dropped unless the gap is filled.

## Shape

- **Route and type.** No type change is needed: a single-commander volume uses `commanderSlug` alone. Proposed: `commanderSlug: "yi-sun-sin"`, giving `/leadership/yi-sun-sin/` and the progress key `leadership/yi-sun-sin`. Title: "Leadership Lessons from Yi Sun-sin: from Okpo to Noryang, 1592–1598". Parts: Orientation; The first year; Rivals and the court; With almost nothing; The end.
- **Question**, as planned: How do you lead when your own government is the greater danger: by obedience, by evasion, by results, or does the record decide who was in danger? The pass found the evidence to test its premise from both sides. The king's memorandum of the 3rd month of 1597 accused Yi of crimes deserving death, and in the 7th month the same king blamed the commander-in-chief's pressure, not Won Gyun alone, for the fleet's loss. The court's danger is also told by partisans: the Annals' historians of 1598, who called Won Gyun greedy and cruel, and Yi's nephew, whose _Record of Conduct_ is the source of the famous sayings.
- **Twelve lessons**, not thirteen: lesson 8 (`without-rank`) is folded into lessons 6 and 9 (correction 1).
- **Eight studies** are available to the lessons.

## Sayings traced

| Saying or story                                | Result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "I still have twelve warships"                 | Traced to the _Record of Conduct_ by his nephew Yi Bun, not to a memorial read in the original or to the Annals. Yi Bun says that when the court, thinking the navy too weak, ordered Yi to fight on land, Yi replied in a memorial that for five or six years the navy had kept the Japanese out of Jeolla and Chungcheong, that he still had twelve warships, and that while he lived the enemy would not despise the navy. Use only with its source; `lib/validation.ts` rejects it without Yi Bun or the _Record of Conduct_ |
| His last words                                 | Traced, with three versions of the concealment. The _Record of Conduct_ gives his words that the battle was at its height and his death was not to be announced, and says his eldest son Hoe, his nephew Wan and his servant Geumi hid it. The Annals' historians say his son began to weep and Yi Mun-uk stopped him, covered the body and beat the drum. The _Seikan iryaku_, following _Jingbirok_, says his nephew hid it. The encyclopedia gives the words without naming a source                                          |
| "Those who resolve to die live"                | Traced to Yi's own diary for the 15th day of the 9th month of 1597, where he cites the military classics to his commanders on the eve of Myeongnyang. It is a citation, not his coinage                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| His prayer before Noryang                      | The _Record of Conduct_ says that on the night before the battle Yi prayed that if this enemy were destroyed he would die without regret; the encyclopedia repeats it. A family source, written after his death                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Undefeated in twenty-three battles             | Not found in any source read. Yi's own report on Busan speaks only of the sorties and engagements of 1592; Ballard says he "never knew defeat". ## Corrections to the plan

1. **Fold lesson 8 into lessons 6 and 9.** Yi's months without rank are told only from his side, in the diary and the _Record of Conduct_, and fit as the end of lesson 6 (the cost of refusing) and the start of lesson 9 (the return). Twelve lessons remain, numbered 0–11: the record; Okpo; the new ship; a way out; what a fleet cannot do; the joint report; the agent's story; the successor; the remnant; the ally; Noryang; the ledger.
2. **The famous sayings are his nephew's record of him.** The twelve ships, the last words and the prayer all come from Yi Bun's _Record of Conduct_. Lessons must name it and treat it as a family memorial, as lesson 0 explains.
3. **Three accounts of who hid his death,** which lesson 11 sets side by side.
4. **The pilot changes with the merge:** lessons 0, 6 and 8 (formerly 9): the record, the agent's story and the remnant.
5. **Read for the pilot:** the Annals entries of the 1st–3rd months of 1597 on the order to intercept Katō and Won Gyun's report; without them lesson 6 rests on the king's memorandum and the encyclopedia.
6. **No Japanese accounts of Sacheon, Danghangpo or Busan** were found; lessons 2–4 say so.
7. **Psychology.** Nothing has been read for this volume. Candidates for the batches, abstracts at least: escalation of commitment (Sleesman and others, 2012, read for the Slim volume, to be re-read) for the court's pressure on Won Gyun in lesson 7; group cohesion (Oliver and others, 1999, likewise) for lesson 8; self-serving attribution (Mezulis and others, 2004, likewise) for the reports in lesson 5. Otherwise `base: "none"`.
8. **Counter-cases proposed** (catalog profiles and studies):
   - Lesson 1: Emmanuel de Grouchy at Waterloo (`emmanuel-de-grouchy`, `waterloo`), who kept to his orders.
   - Lesson 2: Oda Nobunaga at Nagashino (`oda-nobunaga`, `nagashino`).
   - Lesson 3: Hannibal at Cannae (`hannibal-barca`, `cannae`); link the Hannibal volume's `reading-the-consul`.
   - Lesson 4: John Jellicoe at Jutland (`john-jellicoe`, `jutland`).
   - Lesson 5: Titus Labienus, Caesar's lieutenant who went over to Pompey (`titus-labienus`, `pharsalus`); link Caesar's `lieutenants` and `a-rival-who-knew-him`.
   - Lesson 6: Joseph Stilwell's recall in 1944 (`joseph-stilwell`); link the Serbian volume's `after-the-chief`, and Belisarius (`belisarius`) for a commander disgraced and recalled.
   - Lesson 7: Radomir Putnik's crossing into Syrmia in 1914 under allied pressure (`radomir-putnik`, `syrmia-1914`); link the Serbian volume's `when-allies-ask-you-to-attack`.
   - Lesson 8 (the remnant): Tadamichi Kuribayashi at Iwo Jima (`tadamichi-kuribayashi`, `iwo-jima`).
   - Lesson 9 (the ally): Gebhard von Blücher at Waterloo (`gebhard-leberecht-von-blucher`, `waterloo`), an ally who kept his word; link Wellington's `allies-he-could-not-command` and Slim's `last-in-line`.
   - Lesson 10 (Noryang): Thomas J. Jackson at Chancellorsville (`thomas-j-jackson`, `chancellorsville`).
   - Lesson 11: the Serbian volume's `the-ledger-of-survival` or Slim's `the-ledger`.
9. **Rule 5 extended.** The _Record of Conduct_ gives its own ship counts, which do not always match the diary's; none is to be used.
