# Yi Sun-sin: source check for a possible Leadership Lessons volume

**Status (25 November 2026):** a source check only, made before any decision to plan a volume. Nothing in the catalog has changed. `docs/LEADERSHIP_NEXT_VOLUMES_PROMPT.md` lists Yi Sun-sin among the later volumes, with the question "How do you lead when your own government is the greater danger?", and says the volume first needs studies of Okpo, Sacheon, Myeongnyang and Noryang beside `hansan-island`, and a readable translation of his war diary. The Caesar, Hannibal and Scipio, Grant and Lee, and Slim volumes were each chosen ahead of Yi because their main sources could be read in full; this check asks whether that is still true of Yi.

Today Yi's profile links one study, `hansan-island`, which cites the _Encyclopedia of Korean Culture_ (in Korean), two Britannica pages and Kawaguchi Chōju's _Seikan iryaku_ (in Classical Chinese, on Wikisource).

## The finding in brief

The volume can be written, but not in the way the earlier volumes were. No English translation of the diary can lawfully be read online. The diary itself, Yi's battle reports to the court and the Annals of King Seonjo can all be read in full in their original Classical Chinese, and the Annals also in a Korean translation. A volume would therefore rest on Korean and Classical Chinese texts, read and paraphrased with care, with no long quotations of Yi in English. That is the same discipline the Slim volume used for a memoir in copyright, applied to a language barrier instead. The Japanese side is better documented than the catalog now shows. The Ming side is documented least.

## What a volume would need first

1. **Studies.** Yi's profile needs eight linked studies. Studies of his own battles, each with a signed encyclopedia entry and his report or the Annals: Okpo, Sacheon (with Dangpo), Danghangpo, Busan (1592), Myeongnyang and Noryang, which with `hansan-island` make seven. The eighth could be Chilcheollyang (1597), Won Gyun's defeat while Yi was stripped of command, which is the volume's central counter-case but not a battle Yi fought; or Sacheon and Dangpo could be split. That choice is yours.
2. **Reading in the original.** Each study would cite Yi's report or diary entry and the Annals in Classical Chinese or Korean, as `hansan-island` already cites _Seikan iryaku_. Paraphrase, with the language stated; no English "quotations" of Yi made by this project.
3. **Dates.** The Korean sources use the lunar calendar. `hansan-island` keeps lunar dates because no source read converts them; a volume needs either a source that does, or the same rule throughout.
4. **The Ming side.** Not checked here. Chen Lin's biography in the _Ming shi_ and the _Ming shilu_ are the obvious candidates for Noryang.
5. **Source traps to trace before any lesson uses them:** Won Gyun as villain (Aston, Hulbert) against his rehabilitation; the "twelve ships" and the iron chain at Myeongnyang; Yi seeking death at Noryang; the turtle ship as an ironclad; and the "undefeated in twenty-three battles" count. The volume's question also invites a modern political reading of Yi as a hero wronged by his government, which the Annals must test rather than assume.

## Recommendation

A Yi volume is feasible on lawful, readable sources, but slower than any volume so far, because its core texts are in Classical Chinese and Korean. If you want to go ahead, the next step is a Phase S as for Slim: propose the seven or eight studies, read Yi's report and the Annals for each, and only then plan the lessons. If a lawful English translation of the diary matters more than speed, the alternative is to wait and take another subject first.
